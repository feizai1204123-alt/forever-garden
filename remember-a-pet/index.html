Ways to honour a pet who has passed

How to remember a pet that died

There is no right way to do this, and no timetable. Some people want a ritual. Some want a quiet corner. Some only want to say the name out loud again.

Below are gentle, practical ideas that have helped other people. Take what fits, and leave the rest. If the grief feels too heavy to carry alone, there is also free support in the UK.

Ideas you can try, in your own time

  1. 1. Make a small photo book

    Not only the “best” pictures. Ordinary days matter too: a nap in a square of sun, a walk you always took, the way they waited by the door. A simple album on a shelf is enough.

  2. 2. Plant something in their memory

    A tree, a rose, or a pot of herbs on a windowsill. Looking after it can be a way of looking after the love that is still there, especially if you do not have a garden.

  3. 3. Keep a paw print, a lock of fur, or their collar

    A clay or ink paw print, a clipping of fur, a tag or a worn collar — something you can hold when you miss them. If you did not keep one, a favourite blanket or bowl can serve the same purpose.

  4. 4. Write them a letter

    Say the things you did not get to say, or the funny things you already miss. You can keep the letter, bury it, or read it aloud. You do not have to share it with anyone.

  5. 5. Mark a day that belonged to them

    On their birthday, or on the day they left, light a candle and sit with the memory for a few minutes. It can be as small as a quiet cup of tea, or as formal as you like.

  6. 6. Donate in their name

    A gift to a shelter, a rescue, or a charity that helped them — or one that helps animals like them — can turn some of the love you still have into care for another animal.

  7. 7. Tell a story to someone who knew them

    Saying their name out loud still matters. A friend, a family member, a neighbour, or a vet who looked after them may be glad to hear a story, and to tell you one of theirs.

  8. 8. Make a simple keepsake

    A drawing, a pressed flower from a walk you used to take, a stitch of embroidery, a tag on your keys. It does not need to be bought, or beautiful, to be theirs.

  9. 9. Help another animal when you feel ready

    Walk a neighbour’s dog, volunteer, or foster. There is no rush, and it is all right if you are not ready. Honouring one life does not require you to open the door to another before you can.

  10. 10. Visit a quiet place made for remembering

    It might be a bench, a stretch of path you used to walk together, or a small garden online. Forever Garden is one of those places: a free 3D memorial garden where a photo of your pet can become a little figurine, and where visitors leave flowers and candles for one another.

A small garden, if you would like one

Forever Garden is free, with no sign-up. You can walk the paths even if you never upload a photo. If you do, only a few new figurines can be made each day, so that the garden can stay free.

If you need someone to talk to

Grief after a pet dies is real, and you do not have to explain why it hurts. These services are not part of Forever Garden; they are listed because they are there to help.

In the UK, Blue Cross Pet Loss Support (sometimes still called the Pet Bereavement Support Service) is free and confidential, every day from 8.30am to 8.30pm. You can call 0800 096 6606, email plsmail@bluecross.org.uk, or use the webchat on their page.

If you need to talk at any hour, Samaritans are available on 116 123.

The Ralph Site also has clear, gentle information about pet loss, and a community of people who have been through it.
